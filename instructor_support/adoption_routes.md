# Course Adoption Routes

Parts I-III (Chapters 1-12) form a complete CS1 route. Parts IV-V (Chapters 13-20) supply the CS2 data-structures foundation. Parts VI-VII (Chapters 21-26) provide selectable algorithmic depth according to course goals and available time. The book supports COB, IST, IT, IS, and LIS programs as well as other programming-foundations courses; a standalone CS1 course needs no commitment to a second semester.

## Standalone CS1

Use Parts I–III: programming, data and file handling, then object-oriented design. Retain computing context, society, ethics, testing, and information-system examples; the text teaches computing foundations as well as syntax. The database/bookstore inventory appendix is an optional application lab.

## CS2 Foundation

Use the eight units in Parts IV-V: ADTs and costs; arrays and linked lists; stacks and queues; searching and basic sorting; trees; priority queues and heaps; hash collections and sets; graphs and traversal. Each chapter provides a 110-minute lesson plan plus assignments. Adapt the weekly calendar to contact hours and projects.

## CS2 with Algorithmic Depth

Select from Parts VI-VII for analysis, recursive design, advanced sorting, greedy optimization, dynamic programming, and further graph/string topics. Selection should follow learning outcomes and prerequisites rather than a program acronym. A COB or information program may stop after Part V or include useful selections such as analysis and sorting; a course emphasizing algorithms can go further.

## Editorial Direction

The aim is a dependable open foundation text across COB, IST, IT, IS, and LIS. Strengthening Parts VI-VII against dedicated DS/algorithms books is a later editorial pass. The present change expands and assesses the common Parts IV-V foundation, without making Parts VI-VII mandatory.

## Opening Chapter and Setup

Chapter 1 integrates computing concepts, responsibility, and the first saved program. Complete Appendix B setup before the local project meeting. Ethics is reinforced through optional software-failure cases in Chapter 6, privacy/security practice in Chapter 9, and professional collaboration in Appendix C. The first chapter does not require advanced privacy coding or SQL. A complete CS1 route still ends with OOP in Chapter 12.

## Optional Application Capstones and AI Literacy

Use the database appendix after OOP as an optional one-to-two-week business application capstone or persistence milestone. It is not needed for the CS1 or CS2 core route. The bookstore workflow covers relational data, parameters, constraints, persistence, and an optional transaction extension.

AI literacy appears in Chapter 1. Prepared review activities in Chapters 5, 6, and 9 teach contract reading, counterexample selection, test independence, schema specification, and prompt privacy. Use them within existing practice time. Students may use the prepared examples without a live service; permitted live assistance is an additional source of proposals. Continue assessing independent tracing, explanation, code modification, and tests.

AI-powered applications are an optional post-OOP project extension. Require a recorded-response demo mode and a working non-AI core, so provider availability, cost, or account access cannot prevent assessment. Do not require model training or a broad machine-learning survey in this book.

## Reliability Progression

Introduce diagnostics and expected-output checks in Chapters 1-2, repeatable method tests in Chapter 5, and decision boundaries and zero/one/many loop cases in Chapters 3-4. Chapter 6, Testing, Debugging, and Error Handling, consolidates these skills and basic exceptions. Revisit file failures and cleanup in Chapter 9 and object-state/collaboration tests in Chapters 10-12. Chapter 11 owns deeper nullability as supporting reading; retain basic missing-input checks earlier. Planning is updated, but the early-practice and assessment alignment pass remains pending.
