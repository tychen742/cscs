# Course Adoption Routes

Parts I–III (Chapters 1–13) form a complete CS1 route. Part IV (Chapters 14–21) supplies the CS2 data-structures foundation. Instructors can select Part V (Chapters 22–27) for additional algorithmic depth according to course goals and available time. The book supports COB, IST, IT, IS, and LIS programs as well as other programming-foundations courses; a standalone CS1 course needs no commitment to a second semester.

## Standalone CS1

Use Parts I–III: programming, data and file handling, then object-oriented design. Retain computing context, society, ethics, testing, and information-system examples; the text teaches computing foundations as well as syntax. The database/bookstore inventory appendix is an optional application lab.

## CS2 Foundation

Use the eight units in Part IV: ADTs and costs; arrays and linked lists; stacks and queues; searching and basic sorting; trees; priority queues and heaps; hash collections and sets; graphs and traversal. Each chapter provides a 110-minute lesson plan plus assignments. Adapt the weekly calendar to contact hours and projects.

## CS2 with Algorithmic Depth

Select from Part V for analysis, recursive design, advanced sorting, greedy optimization, dynamic programming, and further graph/string topics. Selection should follow learning outcomes and prerequisites rather than a program acronym. A COB or information program may stop after Part IV or include useful selections such as analysis and sorting; a course emphasizing algorithms can go further.

## Editorial Direction

The aim is a dependable open foundation text across COB, IST, IT, IS, and LIS. Strengthening Part V against dedicated DS/algorithms books is a later editorial pass. The present change expands and assesses the common Part IV foundation, without making Part V mandatory.
