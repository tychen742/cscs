# Chapter Organization: Variables, Types, Operators, and I/O

## Instructional role

Extend Chapter 1's literal calculations into a named-data office-supply invoice. Students have seen sequential output but have not learned branches, loops, methods, or class design. Keep examples within that prerequisite boundary; bit operations and solution-level concerns are optional.

## Teaching sequence (110 minutes)

| Notebook | Minutes | Focus |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Variables | 25 | Declaration and definite assignment, state tracing, stale totals, names, var, constants, and local scope |
| Types | 35 | Data meaning, numeric range and overflow, decimal precision and rounding, UTF-16 and strings, assignment semantics, and conversions |
| Operators | 25 | Arithmetic grouping, quotient/remainder reconstruction, conversion timing, updates, comparisons, and short-circuit rules |
| Input and Output | 25 | Prompt/read/parse, input contracts, culture, interpolation/composite formatting, and aligned reports |

Retain four existing notebooks because input/output is a distinct practical workflow and existing cross-references target it. These are four sections across three class meetings, not four required meetings. Use the lab's invoice development for supervised practice between explanations.

## Outcomes

Students can trace and recalculate state, choose types from business meaning, explain finite range and precision, parse known-valid input, distinguish input assumptions from validation, and produce a rounded invoice with matching displayed components.

## Assignments and verification

Preview has ten MCQs. Lab has five connected office-supply tasks, including an input-driven revision and zero/packing/shipping boundary checks. Homework has five applied true/false questions and five coding problems. All cells are independent; input-reading examples include recorded sample input for verification. Answers retain verified stdout.

## Deferred topics

Branching and validation recovery belong in Chapters 4 and 6; loops in Chapter 5; mutable-object sharing and class design in later chapters. Author slides after content completion as book content. Press handles delivery and access controls.
